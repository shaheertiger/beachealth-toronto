Dry Needle

Beachealth Services > Dry Needle

Dry needling at Beachealth targets tight, painful muscle knots directly, helping release tension and get you moving comfortably again.

Dry needling uses very fine, sterile, single-use needles inserted into myofascial trigger points — tight bands in a muscle that can cause local pain, refer pain elsewhere, and restrict movement.

Releasing these points can reduce pain and muscle tension and improve how freely you move. It is usually one part of a broader treatment plan alongside hands-on therapy and exercise.

To book an appointment you can call us at (416) 546-4887 or book online.

How Dry Needling Works

Your clinician locates the trigger points involved in your symptoms, then inserts a fine needle into the tight muscle band.

Treatment may include:

  • Trigger point release: Needling taut muscle bands, which often produces a brief “twitch” as the muscle releases.
  • Combined hands-on care: Pairing needling with manual therapy to restore movement.
  • Movement & exercise: Exercises to keep muscles moving well and reduce the chance of tension returning.

Conditions We Commonly Help With

  • Neck and shoulder tension
  • Low back pain
  • Tension headaches
  • Muscle knots and trigger points
  • Sports-related muscle tightness
  • Hip and buttock pain
  • Tennis elbow and forearm tightness
  • Persistent muscle pain

Dry needling is always optional. If needles are not for you, your clinician can use other techniques.

What It Feels Like

The needles are much finer than those used for injections, so insertion is usually barely felt. When a trigger point releases, you may feel a brief twitch or deep ache, which settles quickly.

Mild soreness, similar to after a workout, is common for a day or two afterward. Your clinician screens your health history first and will let you know if dry needling is not a good fit — for example with certain bleeding conditions or during pregnancy.

What to Expect at Your First Visit

1 Assessment

We discuss your symptoms and health history and assess movement and muscle tension.

2 Consent & Treatment

Your clinician explains the process, answers questions, and needles the relevant trigger points.

3 Movement & Aftercare

Follow-up movement or exercises, plus guidance on what to expect over the next day or two.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is dry needling the same as acupuncture?

Both use fine needles, but dry needling is based on modern anatomy and targets specific muscle trigger points to relieve pain and tension, rather than traditional acupuncture points.

Does dry needling hurt?

Most people feel little during insertion. A muscle twitch or deep ache can occur when a trigger point releases, but it passes quickly.

Are the needles sterile?

Yes. We only use sterile, single-use needles that are disposed of after each treatment.

How soon will I feel a difference?

Some people feel looser straight away; others notice the benefit after the initial soreness settles a day or two later.
