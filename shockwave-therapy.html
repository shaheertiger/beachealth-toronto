How Shockwave Therapy Works

After an assessment to confirm shockwave therapy is suitable, a handheld applicator is placed over the painful area and pulses are delivered for a few minutes.

Treatment may include:

  • Assessment & suitability screening: Confirming your diagnosis and checking that shockwave therapy is safe and appropriate for you.
  • Targeted shockwave therapy sessions: Short treatments over the painful tissue, with intensity adjusted to your comfort.
  • Progressive loading exercises: Strengthening exercises that help the tendon adapt and keep improvements going.
  • Activity & recovery guidance: Advice on managing training, work and daily activity during your treatment course.

Conditions We Commonly Help With

  • Plantar fasciitis and heel pain
  • Achilles tendinopathy
  • Tennis elbow and golfer’s elbow
  • Rotator cuff tendinopathy (including calcific)
  • Patellar tendinopathy (jumper’s knee)
  • Outer hip pain (greater trochanteric pain)
  • Chronic tendon pain in runners and athletes
  • Pain that hasn’t improved with rest

Shockwave therapy is generally used for pain that has lasted several weeks or longer. Your clinician will confirm whether it suits your condition.

Is Shockwave Therapy Right for You?

Shockwave therapy is non-invasive, needs no injections or downtime, and most people continue their normal daily activities. A course commonly involves several sessions spaced about a week apart.

It is not suitable for everyone. Your clinician will screen for situations where shockwave therapy should not be used — for example pregnancy, certain blood-clotting conditions or medications, or treatment over an area with an active infection.

What to Expect at Your First Visit

1 Assessment

2 First Treatment

3 Plan & Exercises

You receive a treatment schedule and exercises to support healing between sessions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shockwave therapy hurt?

It can feel uncomfortable over sensitive tissue, often described as a strong tapping. Intensity is adjusted so it stays tolerable, and each treatment lasts only a few minutes.

How many sessions will I need?

A course commonly involves several sessions about a week apart. Your clinician will recommend a plan based on your condition.

Can I exercise after treatment?

Most people continue normal daily activities. You may be advised to ease off high-impact activity for a short time; your clinician will give you specific guidance.

When will I notice a difference?

Some people notice less pain within a few sessions, while tendon healing continues for weeks after the course ends. Combining shockwave therapy with exercise gives the best long-term results.
